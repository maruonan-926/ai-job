Receiving a job offer is exciting, but deciding whether to accept — especially when you're weighing multiple options — can be genuinely difficult. Compensation, growth trajectory, workload, culture, and brand prestige pull in different directions, and it's easy to overweight the most visible number (base salary) while underweighting factors that will shape your day-to-day experience for years. The Autumn Job Workbench offer evaluator gives you a structured, AI-assisted framework to assess every offer across five consistent dimensions, so your decision is grounded in clear reasoning rather than gut instinct alone.

## Evaluate Your Offer Step by Step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create an offer evaluation for a company and role">
    Navigate to the application record for the role and click **Create Offer Evaluation**, or go directly to **Offer Evaluator** and click **+ New Evaluation**. Enter the company name and the exact role title. Linking the evaluation to an existing application record carries over any notes and JD match data you've already collected.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter salary and compensation details">
    Fill in all compensation components: base salary, target bonus (percentage or fixed), equity (number of units, vesting schedule, estimated current value), signing bonus, and any other cash components. Include the work location and whether the role is remote, hybrid, or on-site — this affects cost-of-living adjustments in the salary competitiveness score.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paste the job description">
    Paste the full job description into the **Role Context** field. The AI uses the JD to understand the seniority level, required responsibilities, and industry segment — all of which inform how it benchmarks compensation and assesses growth potential. The more complete the JD, the more precise the analysis.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let the AI score five dimensions">
    Click **Run Evaluation**. The AI scores the offer across five dimensions:

    - **Salary Competitiveness** — how total cash compares to market benchmarks for the role, level, and location
    - **Growth Potential** — indicators of career advancement based on company stage, role scope, and team structure
    - **Work Intensity** — signals of workload, pace, and expectations drawn from the JD language and company profile
    - **Benefits & Perks** — assessment of non-cash value: health coverage, PTO, learning budgets, retirement matching
    - **Brand & Platform** — the strategic value of the company's name and product portfolio for your long-term career story

    Each dimension receives a score from 1–10 and a weighted overall score.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the AI rationale and risk flags">
    Expand each dimension to read the AI's reasoning. Pay close attention to **Risk Flags** — these are specific signals the AI flagged as potential concerns, such as below-market equity terms, vague growth language in the JD, or unusually high on-call expectations. Risk flags are not deal-breakers by default; they're prompts for further investigation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare multiple offers side by side">
    If you're evaluating more than one offer, all completed evaluations appear in the **Ranked List** view, sorted by overall score. Click **Compare** to open a side-by-side breakdown showing all five dimension scores together. Use this view to identify where offers diverge most sharply and focus your deliberation on the dimensions that matter most to you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  The AI scores salary competitiveness, brand, and growth potential using information from its training data, which has a knowledge cutoff and may not reflect the latest market movements, funding rounds, or layoffs. Always verify important claims — especially compensation benchmarks and company financial health — through independent sources such as Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, LinkedIn, or direct conversations with people in your network.
</Note>

## What to Ask in the HR Round to Fill in the Gaps

A completed offer evaluation will sometimes surface dimensions where the AI had limited information — most commonly Benefits & Perks and Work Intensity. Use the HR or recruiting round to fill those gaps directly. Useful questions include:

- **On benefits:** "Can you walk me through the health plan options and what the employee contribution looks like?"
- **On workload:** "What does a typical week look like for someone in this role during a busy period?"
- **On growth:** "How have people in this role typically progressed within the company over the first two years?"
- **On equity:** "What was the last 409A valuation, and when is the next one expected?"

<Tip>
  Write your questions down inside the offer evaluation record's **Notes** field before the call so you don't forget them under pressure, and log the answers immediately afterward while the conversation is fresh.
</Tip>
